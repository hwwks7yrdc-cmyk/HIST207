The register

Nine accessions

The same four fields that sit beside every work, laid out as the inventory they imitate. Nothing is hidden here. The rooms make you open each one; this page hands you all of them at once, which is a different kind of reading.

Filter by who is permitted to withdraw and the nine works fall into three groups of three. That split is the exhibition’s actual argument, visible here in a way it is not when you walk the rooms one work at a time.

Nine of nine

AccessionArtist and workRoom What is keptWho may withdraw it What was left out
DS – I – 01 Katie Paterson Future Library, 2014–2114 Room I · Deposit One hundred manuscripts, unread, and the thousand trees that will print them. Nobody, until 2114. The Deichman library in Oslo holds them in trust. No one takes anything out Everything those writers wrote on the other three hundred and sixty-four days.
DS – I – 02 Åsa Sonjasdotter Cultivating Abundance, 2022 Room I · Deposit Peasant-bred grain varieties, held in Nordic gene banks. Researchers, and since Allkorn, farmers who ask to grow them again. Only named parties The fields they came from, and the practice of breeding inside them.
DS – I – 03 Amy Balkin A People’s Archive of Sinking and Melting, 2012–ongoing Room I · Deposit Ordinary objects from places expected to disappear. Nothing over a kilogram. No one withdraws. The whole archive is always shown together. No one takes anything out Almost nothing. Two submissions were refused for misreading the brief.
DS – II – 01 Uriel Orlow Theatrum Botanicum, 2015–2018 Room II · Custody Names that plants carried before European botany renamed them. Anyone, in principle. The renaming made the older names hard to find. Anyone may take it The Latin binomial got a herbarium sheet. The older name got none.
DS – II – 02 Vivien Sansour Palestine Heirloom Seed Library, 2014–ongoing Room II · Custody About forty-seven heirloom varieties, in jars, coated in ash. Anyone who comes to the house in Battir. No record is kept. Anyone may take it No accession list exists, so nothing can be audited, including loss.
DS – II – 03 Futurefarmers Seed Journey, 2016–ongoing Room II · Custody Seed grain, in the hold of an 1895 rescue boat. Every port the boat reaches. The cargo is unloaded and discussed. Anyone may take it The fixed itinerary. The crew is allowed to change the route.
DS – III – 01 Maria Thereza Alves Seeds of Change: A Floating Ballast Seed Garden, Bristol, 2012; series begun 1999 Room III · Thaw Seeds carried as ship ballast and tipped out in foreign harbours. The artist, by excavating the mud and letting it germinate. Only named parties Any record of what the ballast held. The garden is now the record.
DS – III – 02 Precious Okoyomon Earthseed, MMK Frankfurt, 2020 Room III · Thaw Living kudzu, soil and sugar cane, in a museum for the run of a show. The plant withdraws itself. It grows over whatever is nearest. No one takes anything out Any plan for what the institution does when the exhibition closes.
DS – III – 03 Kapwani Kiwanga Flowers for Africa, 2013–ongoing Room III · Thaw Floral arrangements from the ceremonies at which countries became independent. A florist in each host city, working from the archival image. Only named parties The protocol forbids maintenance. What you see depends on when you arrive.

Nothing in the collection matches both filters.

A request for any of these can be written on the withdrawal slip. This site keeps no record of who asks, which is the one thing a real vault would never allow.
