Room III

Thaw

What returns, what escapes, what refuses to be kept.

Tsing calls them unruly edges: the margins around managed space where species collaborate without anyone's permission. She argues that these zones of neglect keep producing more life than the managed ground does. This room is the edge. Material that nobody preserved turns out to have survived, and material that was managed gets away.

  • Precious Okoyomon, Earthseed, MMK Frankfurt, 2020

DS – III – 01 Room III · Thaw

Maria Thereza Alves

Seeds of Change: A Floating Ballast Seed Garden, Bristol, 2012; series begun 1999

Born 1961 in São Paulo; lives and works in Berlin and Naples.Barge, ballast soil, germinated plants. Made with the designer Gitta Gschwendtner.

Sailing ships carried soil, sand and rubble as ballast, taking it on in one port and tipping it out in the next. Alves locates that soil in harbour cities, excavates it, and germinates whatever is still dormant inside. Seeds that have waited two or three hundred years come up.

For Bristol she worked with the designer Gitta Gschwendtner to build a garden on a barge moored in the harbour, planted entirely from ballast seed, so that the flora growing there is a record of the city’s shipping routes and of the trade that ran along them.

Why this roomThaw in its most literal form. Nobody deposited these seeds and nobody kept them. They survived in the dark by accident and returned as evidence, which is what Tsing means when she says that zones of neglect keep producing what no collection could.

The work online: mariatherezaalves.org

The label DS – III – 01

What is kept
Seeds carried as ship ballast and tipped out in foreign harbours.
Who deposited it
Nobody. The soil was waste, and the seeds happened to be in it.
Who may withdraw it
The artist, by excavating the mud and letting it germinate.
What was left out
Any record of what the ballast held. The garden is now the record.

Precious Okoyomon

Earthseed, MMK Frankfurt, 2020

Born 1993 in London; raised in Ohio and Nigeria; lives and works in New York.Living kudzu, soil, sugar cane, water, lamb’s wool sculptures.

Okoyomon filled a museum with kudzu, a vine brought from Japan to the United States and planted across the South to hold eroding soil in place. It did not stay where it was planted. It now covers abandoned land across the region and is treated as a pest.

In the gallery the vine was allowed to grow over walls and floor for the run of the exhibition, through soil and sugar cane, around figures made of lamb’s wool. The plant was the artwork and also the thing the institution had to keep alive and could not fully control.

Why this roomA species introduced to solve a problem, which then escaped the terms of its introduction. Okoyomon reads that history alongside the forced movement of people, and lets the plant take the building. It is the clearest case of the feral in the exhibition.

The work online: mmk.art

The label DS – III – 02

What is kept
Living kudzu, soil and sugar cane, in a museum for the run of a show.
Who deposited it
Introduced to the United States from Japan, to hold eroding soil.
Who may withdraw it
The plant withdraws itself. It grows over whatever is nearest.
What was left out
Any plan for what the institution does when the exhibition closes.

Kapwani Kiwanga

Flowers for Africa, 2013–ongoing

Born 1978 in Hamilton, Canada; lives and works in Paris.Fresh floral arrangements made to a written protocol. Sixteen to date.

Kiwanga searches archives for photographs of the ceremonies at which African countries became independent, and looks at the flowers. She identifies each arrangement, takes the image to a florist in the exhibiting city, and has it recreated as closely as the record allows: a buttonhole, a table bouquet, an archway.

The protocol then states that the flowers must not be maintained. They wilt and collapse over the run of the show, and what a visitor sees depends entirely on when they arrive.

Why this roomAn archive that cannot be kept. Kiwanga reconstructs a document and then lets it decay on schedule, which is the exact opposite of the cold chain. It closes the exhibition by asking what preservation was ever promising.

The work online: goodman-gallery.com

The label DS – III – 03

What is kept
Floral arrangements from the ceremonies at which countries became independent.
Who deposited it
The photographer who happened to record the room, and the archive that filed it.
Who may withdraw it
A florist in each host city, working from the archival image.
What was left out
The protocol forbids maintenance. What you see depends on when you arrive.
